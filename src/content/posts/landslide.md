I stumble outside on a cold winter morning. A chilling gust pierces my cheeks like needles pressed against a soft fabric. Tender eyelids struggle to peel open, robbed of a full night of rest, and the relentless wind discourages them even further. I instinctively reach for the pockets of my jacket, but today they feel like a poor consolation in the presence of this harsh weather. 

Why did I have to be awake when even the sun itself wasn't ready?

My senses are attacked in an overwhelmingly unpleasant manner from every direction, like Caesar when he entered the Senate on that fateful day in March. Yet, unlike Caesar, I feel so alive. Unpleasant, but still alive — with an awareness that rarely manifests itself when stuck in the mundanity of everyday life. After reaching my mom's car, I wait impatiently at the passenger door. Despite sharing this tiny blue planet with billions of my kind, I stand here utterly alone under the vast night sky — the monotony of darkness only broken up by the occasional stars speckled across the endless expanse above. As silence fills the air, my mind starts to race. 

I begin to dread going to school. I can't even remember the last time it felt normal. More than anything, I wish to stay in this peaceful moment forever. Suddenly, being exposed to the raw and unforgiving elements feels preferable to my inevitable future. My thoughts wander to fantasies of romance, of opulence, of recognition... of an older me who has everything figured out. 

That will happen right? 

*beep* *beep*

My ruminations end abruptly as my mom exits the house and unlocks the car door. I reluctantly scramble inside as I wait for her to do the same. She puts the key into the ignition and starts the car. I quickly turn on the heat and then my favorite radio station. Familiar tunes play as I doze off. 

---

On the drive home from work, at the red light prior to arriving at my apartment building, the song "Landslide" plays from my Spotify playlist. I listen closely as the melody guides me up the garage and into a parking spot. Instead of hastily getting out of the car as I would typically do, I take a moment to let the song finish. Afterward, I sit and listen to it a few more times.

It's strange how you can experience a place, a book, a movie, or in this specific instance a song so many times, but one day, out of nowhere, it grabs you out of your self-trained apathy and places you uncomfortably in the present. Various emotions and feelings wash over me as I listen: nostalgia, anxiety, regret, forgiveness, and the like. I don't usually let them engulf me for so long, but maybe it's okay to let them simmer once in a while. And, for what it's worth, what a beatiful song "Landslide" is. 

The song captures the bittersweet nature of growth, change, and the passage of time. It exemplifies a core aspect of the human condition: that the beauty of life is inseparable from the temporalness of it. It contains themes of finding strength when presented with life's inevitable challenges, and of facing similar challenges again and again with the same strength — especially when it is difficult. And suddenly, on a work day no different from any other, while sitting alone in my car listening to a beautiful song, I find myself worrying again like that innocent kid did, on a cold winter morning so many years ago. 

Because despite appearing a grown man on the outside, on the inside, I'm still that same lost kid searching for happiness. That same lost kid who often takes steps in the wrong direction. That same lost kid who, in his own young, dumb, and half-assed way, still continues searching.
