I stumble outside on a cold winter morning. A chilling gust pierces my cheeks like needles pressed against a soft fabric. Tender eyelids struggle to peel open, robbed of a full night of rest, and the relentless wind discourages them even further. I instinctively reach for the pockets of my jacket, but today they feel like a poor consolation in the midst of this harsh weather. 

Why did I have to be awake when even the sun itself wasn't?

My senses are attacked in an overwhelmingly unpleasant manner from every direction, like Caesar when he entered the Senate on that fateful day in March. Yet, unlike Caesar, I feel so alive. Unpleasant, but still alive — with an acute awareness of my existence that rarely manifests itself when stuck in the mundanity of everyday life. After reaching my mom's car, I wait impatiently at the passenger door. Despite sharing this tiny blue planet with billions of my kind, I stand here utterly alone under the vast night sky — the monotony of darkness only broken up by the occasional stars speckled across the endless expanse above. As silence fills the air, so do my thoughts. 

I begin to dread going to school. I can't remember the last time it felt normal. More than anything, I wish to stay in this peaceful moment forever. Suddenly, being exposed to the raw and unforgiving elements feels preferable to my inevitable future. My mind wanders to fantasies of romance, of opulence, of recognition... of an older me who has everything figured out. 

That will happen right? 

*beep* *beep*

My ruminations end abruptly as my mom exits the house and unlocks the car door. I reluctantly scramble inside as I wait for her to do the same. She puts the key into the ignition and starts the car. Without skipping a beat, I turn on the heat and then my favorite radio station. Familiar tunes play as I doze off. 

---

It was an unassuming Monday. 

After work, I pack up my things, take the elevator down, and scan my badge to exit the building. The lady at the front desk says, "Have a great day!", so I respond quietly, "You too." Once I find my car in the parking lot, I climb inside, press the ignition button, and plug my phone in. Driving feels empty without music. I take a right turn to leave my company's parking lot, and then a u-turn onto the highway. As I enter, I merge two lanes to the left. It's simpler this way. While driving, instead of pressing the brakes, I let go of the gas pedal so my car gradually slows down. Gas has been quite expensive recently. I take the highway exit and stop at the red traffic light right before my apartment building. 

It was an unassuming Monday. 

As I sit there at the red light, I wonder to myself, "What should I have for dinner?" I am still deep in thought when the song "Landslide" comes up on my playlist. Suddenly, I am no longer that invested in what I'll have for dinner. I listen attentively to the melody and lyrics as they guide me up the apartment garage and into a parking spot. Instead of hastily getting out of the car as I would typically do, I take a moment to let the song finish. Afterward, I sit and listen to it a few more times.

It's strange how you can experience a place, a book, a movie, or in this specific instance a song so many times, but one day, out of nowhere, it grabs you out of your apathy and places you uncomfortably in the present. I can't remember the exact sequence of memories or thoughts that flashed into my head as I sat in the car that day, but I do recall the many emotions and feelings that followed: nostalgia, anxiety, regret, forgiveness, and so much more. I don't usually let them engulf me for so long anymore. But, in that moment, I decided to let them simmer. My problems weren't solved, not in the slightest, but for what it's worth, what a beautiful song "Landslide" is. 

The song captures the bittersweet nature of growth, change, and the passage of time. It exemplifies a core aspect of the human condition: that the beauty of life is inseparable from the temporalness of it. It contains themes of finding strength when presented with life's inevitable challenges, and of facing similar challenges again and again with the same strength — especially when it is difficult. So, on a Monday no different from any other, in a brief moment of clarity while sitting alone in my car appreciating a beautiful song, I found myself worrying again like that innocent kid did, on a cold winter morning so many years ago. 

Because, despite appearing a grown man on the outside, on the inside, I'm still that same lost kid searching for happiness. That same lost kid who often takes steps in the wrong direction. That same lost kid who, in his own young, dumb, and half-assed way, still continues searching nonetheless.
